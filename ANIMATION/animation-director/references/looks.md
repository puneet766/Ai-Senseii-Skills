# Looks that work in code

When the person has no example, pick ONE look for them from this page: decide yourself, don't send a menu. Describe it in plain words, and never by naming a film, studio, game or artist. Each entry says what makes the look work in code, so it can go straight into THE LOOK and BUILD RULES.

| Look | Good for | What makes it work | Draw it with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Soft clay** | Cute characters, small stories | Rounded shapes, one warm lamp, soft shadow under everything, a little surface dent | WebGL |
| **Paper cut-out** | Stories, explainers | Flat layers with a soft drop shadow between them, slight paper grain, slight stepping in the motion (12 pictures a second) | Canvas or SVG |
| **Ink wash** | Calm, serious, ancient | Black ink on warm paper, brush edges that bleed, lots of empty space | Canvas with noise |
| **Golden hour film** | Emotional, cinematic | Low sun behind the subject, long shadows, haze, a dark edge around the frame, fine grain | WebGL |
| **Night neon** | Tech, city, energy | Near-black ground, one or two glowing colours, soft bloom around lights, wet reflections | WebGL |
| **Clean studio** | Products, logos, apps | White or pale ground, one accent colour, soft layered shadows, slow precise moves | CSS or SVG |
| **Blueprint** | How things work | Thin white lines on deep blue, parts drawn as they're named, measured and tidy motion | SVG |
| **Kinetic type** | Quotes, lists, hooks | Words are the picture, one big word at a time, moves timed to a beat | CSS or SVG |
| **Oil painting** | Faith, history, drama | Thick visible strokes, rich dark colour, slow light change, almost still | WebGL or Canvas |
| **Pixel art** | Games, nostalgia | Small drawing scaled up with no smoothing, limited colours, few animation steps | Canvas |

## Choosing

- Characters are the hardest thing to do well. For a first try, soft clay or paper cut-out hides more than realism does.
- Real light and depth need WebGL. It takes longer and uses more of the limit.
- Flat looks are cheaper and often look more designed. If in doubt, go flat.
- If the idea is sad or serious, avoid bright playful looks. Name that mismatch if the person asks for it, then do what they want.

## Pick by kind of video

| They want to make | Pick | Why |
|---|---|---|
| Explainers, "how it works", school or science topics | **Paper cut-out**, or **Blueprint** for machines and systems | Flat layers stay clear at phone size and are cheap to draw well |
| Tech, AI, apps, product demos, tutorials | **Clean studio** | White space and one accent make screens and steps easy to follow |
| Stories with a character, kids' content, cute or funny clips | **Soft clay** if they want depth, **Paper cut-out** if they want it fast | Rounded simple characters hide mistakes that realism shows |
| Motivation, quotes, hooks, lists, news | **Kinetic type** | The words are the picture, so nothing else has to be drawn |
| Finance, business, data, charts | **Clean studio** with a dark ink colour | Numbers need calm space and one colour that means "look here" |
| Faith, history, poetry, calm or serious topics | **Ink wash** or **Oil painting** | Slow, quiet looks carry weight |
| Gaming, retro, memes | **Pixel art** | Few colours and few steps look right on purpose |
| City, music, nightlife, sci-fi | **Night neon** | Glow on near-black reads as energy |
| Travel, nature, emotional stories | **Golden hour film** | Low warm light does half the feeling for you |

If their answer fits two rows, pick the flatter look. If it fits none, pick **Paper cut-out**: it is the easiest to keep the same across many videos.
